# Persona review — Amara Okafor

33, Nigerian-British, PM in Berlin four years. First German tax return. Five months of ALG I last year.
I did this walkthrough the way I'd actually do it: standing on the U8, thumb only, slightly annoyed that I left it this late.

Short version up front: the tone of this app is genuinely good — better than anything else I've been shown for German tax. The *structure* of the trust is not there yet. It talks to me warmly and then asks me to hand over my passport, my IBAN and my legal signature without ever telling me who's accountable, what I'm paying, or what I'm actually approving. Warmth is not the same thing as trust, and I can tell the difference.

---

## 1. Where I hesitated, squinted, or felt patronised

**w1 — Welcome.** "An expert files your German taxes. In English." is the best sentence in the whole app. But immediately under it: *"First mandatory filing? A qualified Steuerberater handles everything — you just share a few documents."* Two problems. "First mandatory filing?" is a nice hook but it's also the first time the word *mandatory* appears and it's phrased as a casual aside, which is the opposite of how it lands in my stomach. And "you just share a few documents" — the word **just**. Every consumer product says "just". It's always a lie. I ended up sharing five things, one of which was a photo of my passport. That's not "just".

Also: which company is this? There's a green check-badge and no logo, no name, no "Taxfix". I'm looking at a screen that says "give us your German taxes" from an anonymous green tick.

**w2 — Liability check.** I hit the ambiguity on question one. My 2025: unemployed until March, then employed. Options are *Employed all year / Employed, with a gap / Self-employed / Student*. I picked "Employed, with a gap" but I genuinely paused — my gap was at the *start* of the year and I was on benefits, not "gapping". There's no *"None of these"* and no *"It's complicated"*. For an app whose whole pitch is expats with messy years, four tidy boxes is a weird bet.

Two smaller squints on this screen: there is **no back arrow**. Six progress dots at the top for what I was told is three questions, so I don't know how long this is. And the round green outline circles read as *multi*-select to me — I tapped and it advanced, which was fine, but I wasn't sure until it happened.

**w5 — Result.** *"That's the rule — and it's routine, not trouble."* This is the best copy line in the product. It answered my number-one fear in nine words. Genuine credit.

Then: *"Deadline 31 July — plenty of time with an expert."* It is **not** plenty of time and the app has no idea what day it is relative to me. When I got to the end, the Filed screen told me my return went in on **31 August 2026** — a month *after* the deadline this chip was reassuring me about. So either the chip is lying or the app doesn't care. Either way I caught it, and once I catch a product being cheerfully wrong about a date, I re-read everything else with suspicion.

I was also told there's an FAQ link here ("More questions? We wrote them down."). **It is not on this screen.** I looked. Same on the home screen — I was told there's a footer link to "Questions everyone asks" and I can't see it either. If the expat FAQ is the thing that answers "does this affect my visa", it cannot be hiding.

**c2 — Pricing.** *"€119.99"*. The `.99` is a supermarket price. I am buying a **Steuerberaterin**. Nobody's accountant charges €119.99; that decimal makes an expert service look like an app subscription and it made me trust it *less*, not more. Say €120. Or say €119 incl. 19% MwSt — because right now it doesn't say whether VAT is included, and for a German professional service that's a real question.

*"A named Steuerberaterin — not a chatbot."* I understand why this line exists but it protests too much. The moment you tell me you're not a chatbot, I start looking for evidence you're not a chatbot, and the evidence on the next screen is *a green circle with the letters AW in it*.

What's not on this screen and should be: what happens if my case is more complicated than expected (does the price change?), what's excluded, and any link to terms. Half this screen is empty white space. That space could be doing the work of convincing me.

**h1 — Meet Anna.** *"Meet Anna Weber · Steuerberaterin · 11 years of German returns · Replies within 1 business day."* Everything about this is right except that I am meeting a **monogram**. If the entire €120 proposition is "there is a real qualified human", then show me her face and her Steuerberaterkammer registration. I've PM'd a marketplace; I know exactly what a placeholder avatar means, and it means *the human is assigned later, or not at all*.

*"most people finish it in 15 minutes"* — mildly patronising, and unfalsifiable. I don't believe your 15 minutes. I've never seen a "most people finish in X" that was true for someone doing it for the first time in a second language.

**s1 — Home.** Clean. I like the five-stage timeline. But there is **no way to contact Anna from this screen**. Her card is right there — name, role, "replies within 1 business day" — and it isn't tappable to anything. The only "Ask Anna" I ever found in the whole app is buried inside the *"I don't have this"* sheet, i.e. I have to declare failure on a document before the app will let me talk to the human I'm paying for. That is backwards.

Also: *"2 of 5 shared"*. Two of which five? I have to leave home and open the checklist to find out what's outstanding. Put the next action's name on the button — "Continue sharing info" tells me nothing.

**s2 — Checklist.** Best screen in the app. Grouping is right, "Lohnsteuerbescheinigung" as grey secondary under "Annual income statement" is exactly the right way round, and the status pills (Checked ✓ / Sent / To share) are legible at a glance.

One thing bugged me: the group header says *"Deductions — optional, can raise your refund"* and yet the optional item is counted in "5 of 5". So I can't reach a complete state without doing the optional thing. Either it's optional or it's in the denominator. Pick one.

And the first item you ask me for is **Photo ID**. Before that item there is not one word anywhere in this app about where my passport photo is stored, who sees it, or GDPR. I uploaded it anyway because I want this over with — which is exactly the behaviour a serious product should not be relying on.

**s3 — Item detail.** The header renders as **"Annual income statem…"** — truncated. On the single screen whose entire job is telling me *which document to go find*. That's the kind of thing that makes me think nobody senior has opened this build.

I was told there's a *"Where do I find this?"* step-by-step on this screen. There isn't. The actual sourcing help — *"Your employer's HR portal has it, or email payroll — they must provide it"* — is hidden behind **"I don't have this"**. So the help only appears after I've given up. That's the wrong trigger. I don't know that I don't have it; I don't know what it *is*.

*"What it looks like: One page, a grid of numbered boxes."* Lovely idea, undercooked. Show me a blurred thumbnail of the actual document. I will recognise a picture in half a second and a sentence in ten.

**s3-escape — "I don't have this" sheet.** Conceptually my favourite thing in the product. Visually it is **broken**. The line *"No problem — here's the way forward"* is sitting inside a green glow/blur artifact that spills off both edges of the sheet, and the text is dark green on green — barely readable. Underneath, **"Ask Anna"** and **"Not sure this applies to me"** render washed-out and blurry, so they read as **disabled**. I nearly didn't tap them. The one screen in the app whose job is "you are not stuck" looks like the app itself is stuck.

**s4 — Follow-ups.** *"Could you double check the IBAN? The one shared looks incomplete."*

Three reactions, in order. (1) Good — a real human noticed a real thing. (2) Hang on: **why did your form accept an incomplete IBAN?** IBAN has a checksum; validate it at entry and this follow-up never has to exist. You spent a business day of my deadline on something a regex catches. (3) There's no timestamp, no due-by, no link back to the Bank details item, and no way to just re-enter the IBAN from here — only a free-text box. I'd be typing an IBAN into a chat field. That's how digits go missing the second time too.

**s5 — Momentum.** *"That's everything Anna needs to start. The ball is in her court now — next update by Tuesday."* I like "the ball is in her court" a lot; it tells me I'm allowed to stop thinking about this.

But look at the top of that same screen: the timeline still has **"Share your info"** as the live stage, in bold, while the bar underneath says **5 of 5 shared** and the card says everything's in. "Anna prepares" is still grey. The screen contradicts itself. And *"by Tuesday"* — which Tuesday? Give me a date. I'm anxious; I will count days.

**s7 — Review.** This is where I'd stop and message a friend.

*"Approve return"* — and **nowhere on this screen does it say I will be charged €119.99.** The pricing screen promised "Pay only when you approve the return". This is the approving. There is no price, no payment method, no "you'll be charged €X now", no confirmation step. In a consumer product I'd flag that in review as a dark pattern; here I'd flag it as a compliance problem.

*"Every deduction you're entitled to"* — that is a promise nobody can make and I don't believe it. "Deductions Anna found: €2,410" is a number I cannot check.

And I can't check any of it, because **I can't see the return**. There is no PDF, no preview, no line items, no forms. I'm approving a legal filing to the German state by looking at four numbers on a card. Worse: **the ALG-I isn't in "The numbers" at all** — the benefits are the entire reason I'm here, they're why the filing is mandatory, and they're absent from the summary. Nothing about Progressionsvorbehalt, which is the one German word an ALG recipient actually needs explained at exactly this moment.

*"Nothing is filed until you approve. Take your time."* — good, calm, correct. But take my time doing *what*? You've given me nothing to spend the time on.

And the thing I'd Google right there, in the U-Bahn, before tapping the big green button: **if this return is wrong, who pays?** Not answered anywhere in the journey. No Steuerberater liability insurance mention, no professional indemnity, and — the one that would actually stop me — **no Vollmacht**. A Steuerberaterin cannot file on my behalf without a power of attorney. I was never asked to sign one. So either that's missing from the flow, or "Approve return" is quietly doing double duty as a legal authorisation, which is worse.

**s8 — Filed.** *"Filed. Your 2025 return went to Finanzamt Berlin on 31. August 2026."*

Two things. First, **"31. August 2026"** — German date punctuation dropped into English copy. Tiny, but this is the app that sells itself on "In English."

Second, and much bigger: the app told me on three separate screens that my deadline was 31 July and that I was **"on track"**, and then filed a month late and said *"Filed."* with a green rosette and no acknowledgement whatsoever. That is the single most alarming moment in the journey. If I'm late, tell me I'm late, tell me what it means (Verspätungszuschlag? interest? nothing?), and tell me Anna handled it. Silence reads as "they didn't notice", and this is the one area where I cannot afford them not to notice.

Also missing from Filed: a **confirmation/transmission reference**, a **copy of what was actually filed**, and an **invoice** for the €119.99 — which by this point I still don't know whether I've paid. Anna's card sits at the bottom, still not tappable. The relationship just ends. If the Bescheid arrives in 6 weeks and I can't read it, my only route back to Anna is... reopening the app and hoping.

## 2. Where I felt genuinely reassured

- *"That's the rule — and it's routine, not trouble."* Directly disarms "am I already in trouble". Best line in the app.
- *"Pay only when you approve the return"* + *"No payment now. Cancel any time before you approve."* Exactly the right commercial shape for someone who doesn't trust you yet. (Which makes the silent approve screen such a self-inflicted wound.)
- The whole **"I don't have this"** concept. The fact that someone thought about the moment I fail is why I'd give this app the benefit of the doubt at all. *"Anna will confirm either way"* removes the fear of getting it wrong, which is my second-biggest anxiety.
- **German term as the secondary line, never the primary.** "Annual income statement / *Lohnsteuerbescheinigung*". This is the correct hierarchy and almost nobody gets it right.
- **"Sent to Anna — she'll check it."** A named human checks it. That one sentence does more for the €120 than the whole pricing screen.
- The **status pills** — Checked ✓ vs Sent vs To share. I always know whether a human has actually looked.
- **Live sync** — knowing my upload flips a row on her side instantly. That's the thing that makes it feel like a service rather than a form.
- *"The ball is in her court now."* Permission to stop worrying. Rare and valuable.
- The **notification pre-permission** framing: *"Only the nudges that matter — never marketing."* I'd grant that. And *"Most people finish in 15 minutes. Anna's ready when you are."* as the single re-engagement nudge is restrained — one calm reminder, not a drip campaign. That restraint reads as confidence.

## 3. What's missing that I'd expect as a first-time expat filer

Ranked by how likely each is to make me close the app:

1. **Who is liable if it's wrong.** Nowhere. This is anxiety #2 and the product is silent. One line — "Anna is a licensed Steuerberaterin; her professional liability insurance covers the return she files for you" — would do it.
2. **The Vollmacht.** I never signed anything authorising a Steuerberaterin to act for me. Either it's missing or it's hidden inside "Approve return". Both are bad.
3. **A front door to Anna.** No message, no "ask a question", no inbox, no timestamp of her last activity — except buried in a failure sheet. Anxiety #3 completely unaddressed on the surface.
4. **What I'm actually approving.** The return itself. A PDF, a line-item view, *anything*.
5. **Where my passport photo goes.** No privacy line before the very first upload.
6. **What "late" means.** I'd Google "Germany tax return deadline missed penalty" mid-flow and possibly not come back.
7. **Progressionsvorbehalt, at the moment it matters.** Apparently it's in the FAQ. The FAQ isn't reachable from any screenshot I saw. My benefits push my tax rate up on my *other* income; if I discover that from the Bescheid instead of from you, I'll feel misled.
8. **Whether this repeats.** Do I have to do this again next year? Is my ALG year a one-off? I'd Google that at the Filed screen.
9. **Visa/Aufenthaltstitel implications.** Apparently in the FAQ. Same problem.
10. **What happens if Anna doesn't reply.** "Replies within 1 business day" is a promise with no consequence attached. What do I do on day 3? There's no support, no company, no email, no escalation.
11. **A receipt and an invoice.** For a €120 professional service. Also: I might be able to deduct that fee next year.
12. **Any company identity at all.** No logo, no Impressum, no legal entity. Germans and expats both look for an Impressum.

## 4. What would delight me enough to tell another expat

- The **camera scanner that locks onto the document** — corner brackets, shutter, "Use this photo / Retake". Taking a photo of a letter and being done is the whole reason to do this on a phone. That's the demo moment.
- **Anna's row flipping to "Checked ✓" while I'm still holding the phone.** A human, watching, in real time. I'd screenshot that and send it to the Berlin expats WhatsApp group.
- **"I don't have this" → "No problem — here's the way forward."** Every other tax product punishes you for not having the thing. Fix the rendering and this is your most shareable idea.
- **German terms as secondary text.** "It tells you what the scary German word means" is a sentence I would actually say to a friend.
- The **notification when the draft lands: "Your return is ready to review — €1,286."** A number in a push notification, from a person who did the work. That's a nice moment.
- If **"€1,286"** were framed as *"more than the €120 you're paying"* — that's the argument, and you never make it. Make it and I'll evangelise.

## 5. What would make me distrust it or suspect a scam

In descending order of how fast my thumb moves to the App Switcher:

1. **"Approve return" with no price on it.** You told me I'd pay on approval; then you took the payment moment off the screen. That's the exact shape of a subscription trap. Nothing else on this list is as damaging.
2. **Filed a month after the deadline you told me I was "on track" for, with a celebratory tick and no comment.** Either you're not watching my case or you're hiding bad news. Both are fatal for a service I hired to worry on my behalf.
3. **No liability, no Vollmacht, no Impressum, no company name.** An anonymous green badge is asking for my passport, my IBAN and my tax ID.
4. **The initials avatar.** "A named Steuerberaterin — not a chatbot" next to a monogram. If she's real, show her. If she's not assigned yet, say so.
5. **The broken escape sheet.** Green glow bleeding off the edges, unreadable heading, links that look disabled. Sloppy rendering on a financial app reads as *unmaintained*, and unmaintained reads as *nobody's home*.
6. **"Annual income statem…"** truncated in a 34pt headline. Same signal.
7. **A form that accepted an invalid IBAN** — then asked me to fix it by typing it into a chat box. That's my *bank account*. If your validation is that loose, what else isn't checked?
8. **€119.99.** Retail decimals on a professional service.
9. **"Every deduction you're entitled to."** Unprovable superlative on an unviewable document. Overclaiming makes me discount the claims that *are* true.
10. **No email, no account, no confirmation of anything.** If I lost this phone tonight, is there any evidence my German tax return exists?

## TOP 5 CONCRETE ASKS

**1. Let me see the return before I approve it, and tell me who's liable if it's wrong.**
Add a "View your return" step above the button — a real PDF or at minimum line items including the ALG-I and its effect on my rate (Progressionsvorbehalt, in one plain sentence) — plus a permanent line reading "Anna is a licensed Steuerberaterin (Kammer reg. no. …); her professional liability insurance covers this filing", and put the Vollmacht signature in the flow as its own explicit step. *Because I am authorising a legal filing to a foreign government based on four numbers on a card, and right now the app has never once told me who carries the risk if one of them is wrong.*

**2. Put the price on the Approve screen and send me an invoice after.**
"Approving files your return and charges €119.99 (incl. VAT) to your card" above the button, a confirm sheet, and a PDF invoice on the Filed screen. *You earned enormous goodwill with "pay only when you approve" and then removed the price from the exact screen where I approve — that's the one move in this whole app that made me wonder if I was being handled.*

**3. Give me a front door to Anna on the home screen, with a real clock and a real escalation.**
Make her card tappable to "Ask Anna", surface Follow-ups from home with a badge, timestamp her last activity ("Anna checked your ID · 2h ago"), and state plainly what happens if she misses the 1-business-day promise and who I contact instead. *I'm paying €120 for a human, and the only way I found to talk to her was to first admit I couldn't find a document.*

**4. Use real dates everywhere, and be honest when I'm late.**
Replace "Deadline 31 July — plenty of time" with a live countdown against today's date, turn "next update by Tuesday" into "next update by Tue 8 Sept", and if the filing goes in after the deadline the Filed screen must say so and say what it means — "Filed 31 Aug, after the 31 July deadline. Anna filed a late-submission note; no penalty is expected." *Your app cheerfully told me I was on track and then filed a month late without blinking, and that is precisely the failure I hired you to prevent.*

**5. Fix the details that make you look unmaintained — starting before I upload my passport.**
One privacy line above the first upload ("Encrypted, visible only to Anna, deleted on request"); fix the truncated "Annual income statem…" header; fix the green-glow rendering and the washed-out links on the "I don't have this" sheet; validate the IBAN at entry (checksum) and let me re-enter it from the follow-up rather than typing it into a chat box; advance the timeline to "Anna prepares" when it says 5 of 5; drop the optional receipts out of the denominator; put the FAQ link where it was promised on both the result and home screens; give Anna a photo and a registration number; and add a logo and an Impressum somewhere. *Individually these are nitpicks, but together they're the difference between "a firm of tax advisors with an app" and "an app that says it has tax advisors" — and I'm about to give it my passport.*

---

Would I finish this flow? Yes — because the tone is right and "I don't have this" told me someone thought about me on my worst moment. Would I recommend it to the Berlin expat group as it stands? Not until the Approve screen tells me what it's charging me and the Filed screen admits what day it is.
